Understanding Core Web Vitals

What the vitals actually measure

Core Web Vitals are Google's field-measured view of how a page feels: Largest Contentful Paint for how fast the main content arrives, Interaction to Next Paint for how quickly the page responds when someone acts, and Cumulative Layout Shift for whether the page holds still while it loads. They are judged on real visits over a rolling 28-day window, at the 75th percentile — which means your slowest quarter of real experiences is what counts, not your best lab run.

Why they move rankings and revenue

Page experience is a confirmed ranking input, so failing vitals quietly cap what your content can win. The larger effect is commercial: slow, unresponsive, shifting pages shed visitors before the offer is even seen, and every point of friction shows up in conversion long before it shows up in a report. Fixing vitals is therefore both an SEO task and a revenue task — and it is the specialised heart of our website speed optimization services.

What we fix

Metric by metric

Largest Contentful Paint — loading

A failing LCP is usually a delivery problem: a hero image that arrives late, a server that answers slowly, render-blocking resources queuing ahead of the content. We attack the chain in order — server response, resource priority, image format and sizing, critical CSS — until the main content paints inside the 2.5-second threshold on real devices.

Interaction to Next Paint — responsiveness

INP fails when JavaScript hogs the main thread: heavy scripts, long tasks, bloated third-party tags. We profile the interactions your visitors actually make, break up and defer the work blocking them, and remove or re-sequence the scripts that tax every click — responsiveness is an architecture decision, not a setting.

Cumulative Layout Shift — stability

CLS is a discipline problem: images and embeds without reserved space, fonts that swap late, banners and popups that shove content down mid-read. We reserve space for everything dynamic, discipline font loading, and audit third-party injections — a page that holds still is a page people trust.

First Contentful Paint — first impressions

FCP is the moment a blank screen becomes a page. Shortening the critical rendering path — leaner first response, inlined critical styles, deferred non-essentials, sane caching and delivery — makes the site feel fast before it finishes being fast, which is what visitors actually perceive.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

How long until field data improves?

CrUX is a rolling 28-day window — expect visible movement in 2–4 weeks after fixes ship.

Our lab scores pass but field fails. Why?

Real visitors use slower devices and networks than the lab. We optimise for their conditions.

Can every page pass?

Most templates can with the right fixes; the audit flags any that need structural changes and says so honestly.

Does CWV really affect rankings?

It is a confirmed ranking input and a bigger conversion factor — slow, shifting pages lose money either way.

What thresholds count as passing?

LCP within 2.5 seconds, INP under 200 milliseconds and CLS under 0.1 — each measured at the 75th percentile of real visits over 28 days, on mobile and desktop separately.

Why did FID become INP?

First Input Delay only measured the first interaction's delay; Interaction to Next Paint measures responsiveness across interactions, which is what users actually experience. We optimise and report against INP.

Will vitals work break our design or features?

Fixes are staged and tested; where a feature genuinely conflicts with a threshold — an autoplaying hero video, say — we show you the measured trade-off and let the business decide, rather than quietly removing things.
